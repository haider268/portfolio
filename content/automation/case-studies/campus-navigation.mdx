A caller walking across a campus, asking where to go, while their position
changes under the conversation. WebSocket carries control signals; WebRTC data
channels stream live GPS — latitude, longitude, heading, accuracy — in
parallel with the audio itself.

## Fast enough to sit inside a sentence

"How far is Block B" cannot wait on a mapping API. An in-memory
[R-Tree / PostGIS spatial index](/systems/micro-tools) computes walking and
transit durations in under 15 milliseconds, running in parallel with speech
recognition — so the answer is already in context before the model generates
its first token.

## What the field did to the design

This is the project that hardened the [speech loop](/systems/speech-loop).
Every fix below now exists because something real went wrong:

- **Phantom guidance.** Indoor GPS drift past 15 metres produced
  contradictory turn instructions. Now a location confidence gate: past a
  15m accuracy radius the agent stops giving vector distances and switches
  to landmark guidance — "near Block B" instead of "in 40 metres".
- **False barge-in.** A cough cut the agent off mid-sentence. Barge-in now
  requires over 250ms of continuous voice energy plus spectral speech
  verification before the buffer clears.
- **Echo loop.** Speaker audio bled into the mic and the agent transcribed
  itself. Server-side echo cancellation, plus an output mute gate on the
  STT pipeline while synthesis is playing.
- **Mid-sentence target switching.** "Take me to Block B… actually, the
  library." A streaming intent parser invalidates the previous tool result
  the moment a new spatial entity appears — a soft reset that keeps the
  rest of the conversation.

The full set lives in the [failure catalogue](/systems/reliability).
