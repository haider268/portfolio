A multi-location wellness brand was opening a new site, and the opening week is
the only week that behaves like a deadline. Everything before it is
anticipation you can bank; everything after it is ordinary trading. The brand
already ran across several timezones, so "call the leads back" was never one
instruction — it was a different instruction depending on which of them you
meant.

The build had to answer a narrower question than it looks. Not *how do we get
leads* — paid social does that. The question was what happens to a lead in the
minutes after the form is submitted, when there is nobody at a desk and the
person who filled it in is still holding their phone.

## What ran

Leads were generated ahead of opening and held in an educational nurture rather
than pitched to straight away. There was nothing to book yet, and a booking
prompt with nothing behind it would have burned the intent. On launch day the
whole cohort was activated at once.

From there every lead took the same path, and no human touched any of it:

- The form submission entered the CRM, tagged and staged, over a webhook.
- [Middleware validated the record](/systems/pipelines-crm) — phone present and
  parseable, required fields filled — **before** anything tried to dial it.
- Inside sixty seconds a [voice agent](/systems/voice-agents) called.
- On the call it handled the objection, [resolved the caller's
  timezone](/systems/scheduling-timezone), read live availability, booked, and
  sent the confirmation SMS with the address and a cancel link before hanging
  up.
- A [messaging track](/systems/messaging-automation) ran in parallel, gated to
  each lead's own local business hours.
- The booking was written back to the CRM, the pipeline advanced, and the
  client's reporting updated itself.

> The expensive part of answering a lead is the waiting. Waiting is also the
> only part of it a person cannot do at three in the morning.

## The numbers

Around 550 to 600 leads went through it. Forty-two appointments were booked in
the first thirty-six hours. The opening week filled.

The figure that mattered internally was a different one: zero human touches.
Not "reduced admin" — none. A receptionist was never in the path, so there was
no point at which the system's throughput was capped by somebody being awake,
and no shift during which the response time quietly went from one minute to the
next morning.

## What the call actually had to survive

Booking on a live call is not a form with a voice on top. The agent had to hold
turn-taking against a caller who interrupts, decline to answer anything it had
not just read from a tool, recognise a voicemail greeting that sounds like a
person, and get a name spelled correctly without turning the call into a
spelling test.

The timezone piece is the one most builds get wrong, because most builds are
tested in one country and quietly break outside it. Here the lead and the
client each had to see their own correct local time for the same appointment,
and every call and message had to fire inside the lead's business hours rather
than the brand's. That is [its own
subsystem](/systems/scheduling-timezone), not a formatting step.
