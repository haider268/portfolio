A multi-location wellness brand was opening a new site. The opening week is the
only week that behaves like a deadline, and the brand already ran across several
timezones — so "call the leads back" was never one instruction.

The question I had to answer was narrow. Not how to get leads; paid social does
that. What happens to a lead in the minutes after the form is submitted, when
there is nobody at a desk and the person who filled it in is still holding their
phone.

## What ran

<SystemDiagram />

I generated leads ahead of opening and held them in an educational nurture. There
was nothing to book yet. On launch day I activated the whole cohort at once.

Every lead then took the same path, and no human touched any of it:

- The form submission entered the CRM over a webhook, tagged and staged.
- [Middleware validated the record](/systems/pipelines-crm) — phone present and
  parseable, required fields filled — **before** anything tried to dial it.
- Within a minute of that lead's own submission, a
  [voice agent](/systems/voice-agents) called.
- On the call it handled the objection,
  [resolved the caller's timezone](/systems/scheduling-timezone), read live
  availability, booked, and sent the confirmation SMS with the address and a
  cancel link before hanging up.
- A [messaging track](/systems/messaging-automation) ran in parallel, gated to
  each lead's own local hours.
- The booking was written back to the CRM and the pipeline advanced.

<Trace />

## The numbers

About 550 to 600 leads went through it across the launch. Forty-two appointments
were booked in the first thirty-six hours after activation. The opening week
filled.

The sixty-second ceiling is per lead, measured from that lead's own form
submission. It is not a throughput claim.

> The expensive part of answering a lead is the waiting. Waiting is also the
> only part of it a person cannot do at three in the morning.

The figure that mattered internally was zero human touches. Not reduced admin —
none. There was no point at which throughput was capped by somebody being awake.

## What the call had to survive

Booking on a live call is not a form with a voice on top. The agent had to hold
turn-taking against a caller who interrupts, decline to answer anything it had
not just read from a tool, recognise a voicemail greeting that sounds like a
person, and get a name spelled correctly without turning the call into a
spelling test.

Timezones are where most builds break, because most are tested in one country.
Here the lead and the client each had to see their own local time for the same
appointment, and every call and message had to fire inside the lead's hours
rather than the brand's. That is
[its own subsystem](/systems/scheduling-timezone).
