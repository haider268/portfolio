Every send honours the lead's timezone, not the client's. That is the whole
capability, and it rests on a gate that has to sit in front of every outbound
path rather than most of them.

Messaging runs in parallel with the [voice track](/systems/voice-agents), off the
same pipeline event. The call is the attempt to convert now. The messages are
what happens when the call did not connect, or connected and did not close.

## The gate

A send is not "message a person"; it is "message a person, at a moment that is
acceptable to them". So a queued message carries the recipient's resolved zone
from the [scheduling engine](/systems/scheduling-timezone) and is released
against the local clock, not the sender's.

The consequence is that a campaign does not go out at once. It goes out as a
wave that moves west, which looks wrong on a dashboard and is right on a phone.

- Mid-call sends — the address, the booking link, the cancel link — bypass the
  gate. The lead is on the phone; that is definitionally an acceptable moment.
- Scheduled nurture does not. It waits.

## Compliance

STOP flips do-not-disturb automatically, with CRM-level enforcement behind it.
Two mechanisms rather than one: the platform flag and the account flag fail in
different ways, and compliance failures are not the kind you find out about
gently.

Opt-out is honoured across the channel, not the campaign. A person who stops one
sequence has not asked to be moved to another one.

## Tradeoffs

**Parallel versus sequential.** Running messaging alongside the call risks a
text arriving while the phone is ringing. Running it after the call means a lead
who did not answer hears nothing for hours. Parallel wins, with the mid-call
sends owned by the agent so the two tracks are coordinated on the one occasion
they overlap.

**Fewer, later sends.** The gate reduces total volume — some sends slip a day.
It also removes the single fastest way to make a lead hate a brand.

## Failure modes

A lead who replies STOP mid-sequence and is queued for the next one. A message
scheduled before the zone was resolved, which is why an unresolved zone holds
the send rather than defaulting to the client's clock. Duplicate sends from a
retried workflow, which is handled the same way duplicate writes are — see
[reliability engineering](/systems/reliability).
