Revenue-side systems engineering, not marketing. The work here is
infrastructure that happens to point at a pipeline.

## The middleware

A Node.js/Express and Redis ingestion layer sits in front of everything. It
verifies **HMAC SHA-256** signatures, normalises every vendor's payload into
one canonical schema, and holds a 60-second idempotency lock so a retried
webhook cannot double-write. The caller gets its acknowledgement in under
100ms; the real work goes to **BullMQ** in the background — the vendor is
never kept waiting, and nothing is lost if processing is slow.

## Staying inside everyone's limits

- **Token-bucket rate limiters** mapped to each vendor's actual published
  cap — HubSpot at 10 requests a second, and so on.
- **Exponential backoff with full jitter** on retries.
- **A circuit breaker** that fails a primary provider over to a secondary —
  Twilio to AWS SNS — instead of hammering an outage.
- **Dead-letter queues** that raise a Slack alert rather than swallowing the
  failure. The [no-silent-failures rule](/systems/reliability) applies here
  first.

## Before the dial, and after

Twilio **HLR lookup** strips landlines and non-fixed VoIP numbers out of the
dialer queue before a call is ever attempted. A **Clay** waterfall
enrichment layers firmographics, revenue band and tech stack onto the
record, and the score decides the treatment: qualified mobile leads to a
direct dialer, everything else to email nurture.

Downstream, [CRM orchestration](/systems/pipelines-crm) runs across
GoHighLevel, HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho and Pipedrive — tagging, staging,
deduplication, write-back and reporting — and multi-channel
**database reactivation** loops run against dormant lead bases, gated
strictly to each recipient's local business hours.
