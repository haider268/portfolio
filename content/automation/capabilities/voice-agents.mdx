Turn latency is the whole game. A caller does not experience an agent as
accurate or inaccurate; they experience it as fast or awkward. Past roughly a
second and a half of silence they assume the line dropped or that it is their
turn, and they start talking. Once they talk over the agent the conversation
stops being a conversation and becomes two systems interrupting each other.

So the working budget is one to one and a half seconds, end of caller speech to
first audio back. Everything below is downstream of defending it.

## Where the budget goes

The loop is speech-to-text, then a model turn that may call tools, then
text-to-speech, over a telephony leg. Each stage spends some of the budget, and
the two that are easiest to overspend are the ones nobody watches:

- **Endpointing.** Deciding the caller has finished. Set it eagerly and the
  agent talks over people mid-sentence; set it lazily and every single turn
  inherits the delay. This is a per-deployment setting, not a default.
- **Tool round-trips.** A tool call inside a turn spends real time. The
  [timezone resolver](/systems/scheduling-timezone) is built to about 250ms for
  exactly this reason — it runs inside a turn the caller is waiting through.

Streaming is what makes the rest survivable. Speech synthesis starts on the
first complete sentence rather than the finished reply, so the caller hears the
beginning of an answer while the end of it is still being generated.

## What the agent owns

Conversation flow, turn-taking, interruption handling, and knowing when to
stop. Barge-in has to actually cancel synthesis rather than queue behind it.
Voicemail has to be recognised, both from the platform's own detection and from
the shape of the greeting, because a recorded greeting is designed to sound
like a person answering.

Human handoff is a first-class outcome, not a failure branch. An agent that
cannot hand over is an agent that will eventually argue with somebody.

## Tradeoffs actually taken

**Bigger model versus faster model.** Reasoning quality is worth very little at
the far end of a phone line if it arrives after the caller has started talking.
The reply is one or two sentences of spoken English; that is not where model
capability is the constraint.

**Confirm before writing.** Receiving a name and a phone number is not
permission to book. Details arriving and details being confirmed are separate
turns, always — which costs one round trip and removes an entire class of wrong
bookings that are expensive to unwind.

**Never speak from context.** Every stated fact is a live read. This costs
latency on some turns and removes hallucinated availability entirely. See
[reliability engineering](/systems/reliability) for how the writes are made
safe.

## Failure modes it is built against

Voicemail answering like a person. A caller interrupting mid-sentence. Speech
recognition mangling a name or an email — handled by reading back word for word,
but only where a required field would otherwise be stored wrong, because
readback on every field turns a two-minute call into a five-minute one.
