Most people ship an agent and walk away. This is the part that separates a
production system from a demo, and it is unglamorous enough that it is usually
the first thing dropped.

An agent that works in testing works against the conversations you imagined. A
deployed agent meets the ones you did not: the caller who answers a question
with a question, the one who gives a date as "next Thursday, no wait, the one
after", the one whose first word is "is this a robot".

## The loop

Listen to real calls. Find the turn where it went wrong. Change one thing.
Listen again.

The discipline is in the second step. A call that ended badly usually failed
several turns before the point where it sounded bad, and the failure is almost
never the sentence the agent said — it is the state it was in when it said it.

Failures sort into a small number of buckets, and each bucket has a different
fix:

- **Heard wrong.** Speech-to-text produced something plausible and incorrect.
  Fixed at the provider settings, or with a readback on that specific field.
- **Turn-taking wrong.** The agent spoke over the caller, or left a gap long
  enough that the caller spoke first. Fixed at endpointing, not in the prompt.
- **Decided wrong.** The agent had the right information and did the wrong
  thing. This one is the prompt.
- **Was not told.** The agent needed a fact nobody had given it a tool for.
  Fixed by adding the tool, never by putting the fact in the prompt where it
  will go stale.

Sorting a failure into the wrong bucket is how prompts grow to two thousand
words trying to fix a microphone problem.

## Tradeoffs

**One change at a time.** Slower, and it is the only way to know which change
did it. Two edits and an improvement tells you nothing you can keep.

**Tune for the median call, escalate the tail.** There is always a caller the
agent should not be handling. Widening the agent to cover them makes it worse at
the ninety percent it should be excellent at. That caller wants
[a human](/systems/voice-agents), quickly.

**Latency against accuracy, deliberately.** Endpointing and speech settings are
one dial with a cost at both ends. It gets set per deployment against real
recordings, not left at a default.

## What this feeds

Everything else. The [reliability tiers](/systems/reliability) are a list of
things that were found this way; the failure modes on every other capability
