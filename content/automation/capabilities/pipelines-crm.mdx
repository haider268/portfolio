Validation before the dial is the cheapest reliability win in the system, and it
is cheap because of where it sits rather than what it does. The machinery on
this page has booked **more than 1,500 appointments** through CRM pipeline
automation — a separate channel from the
[voice-agent bookings](/work/voice-deployments), additive to them.

A lead form will hand you a phone number with a letter in it, a country code
that is not one, or a required field that the user skipped because the form let
them. If that record reaches the dialler, the system spends a call attempt, a
retry, a queue slot and a log line finding out what a regular expression could
have told it for free. Worse, it produces a contact in the CRM that looks
contactable and is not, which someone will later work through by hand.

So I put the gate in middleware, between the webhook and anything that dials.

## The shape

Everything is event-driven. A form submission is an event; it is not a row
somebody polls for. The chain is: receive, normalise, validate, write, tag,
stage, then emit — and only the emit triggers outbound work.

- **Normalise before validating.** Phone numbers arrive in half a dozen shapes.
  Normalising to E.164 first means one validation rule instead of six.
- **Tag and stage at write time.** Pipeline stage is set as the contact is
  created, not by a later automation that may or may not have run. A record's
  stage should never be a question about which workflows have fired.
- **Emit once.** The downstream [voice](/systems/voice-agents) and
  [messaging](/systems/messaging-automation) tracks both hang off the same
  event, so they cannot disagree about whether a lead exists.

Which CRM matters less than it appears. GoHighLevel is the common one. HubSpot,
Zoho and Pipedrive have the same primitives under different names, and n8n or
Make sit in the middle where native automation runs out of expressiveness.

## Outcome-based routing

The same tag-and-stage machinery, triggered by a second input: not just a
lead *arriving*, but a call *ending*.

Every call result is classified — booked, no answer, voicemail, declined,
call back later — and the classification re-tags and re-stages the contact.
The new stage decides what fires next: an SMS nurture sequence, an email
track, a promotional offer, or a re-attempt at a better hour. A lead that
booked leaves the chase paths automatically; a lead that said "call me next
month" is parked and resurfaced, not re-dialled tomorrow by a workflow that
never heard the call.

The [agent's memory of prior calls](/systems/conversation-memory) reads
from the same records this routing writes — one contact history, consumed
by both.

## Tradeoffs

**Drop versus quarantine.** A record that fails validation is not deleted — it
is written, flagged, and left out of the outbound path. Dropping it entirely
saves a row and loses the ability to answer "how many leads did we actually
get?", which is the first question the client asks.

**Native automation versus middleware.** Platform-native builders are faster to
change and harder to reason about. Anything conditional enough to need a comment
belongs in middleware where it can be read.

**Idempotency at the boundary.** Webhooks get delivered twice. The write is
keyed so a duplicate delivery produces the same contact rather than a second
one — see [reliability engineering](/systems/reliability).

## Failure modes

A webhook delivered twice. A webhook not delivered at all, which is why the
receiving end is retried rather than trusted. A form that changes shape because
somebody edited it in the ad platform, which is caught as a validation failure
rather than as a silent field of empty strings.
